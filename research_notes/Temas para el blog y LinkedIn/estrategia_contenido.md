## 1. LinkedIn algorithm 2025-2026: formats, links, comments, dwell time, "link in first comment", recent changes

### Takeaway
Organic reach on LinkedIn has fallen sharply (roughly half or more for active creators over two years) and distribution shifted from "your connections" to "interest/topic matching" via an LLM-based feed rebuilt in March 2026. Native PDF documents (carousels) are the most consistently top-performing format. Link posts are the weakest format by engagement rate in most benchmarks, but LinkedIn denies an explicit link penalty and one large study even found links add interactions; the "link in first comment" trick has no reliable evidence of helping anymore.

### Cited Findings
**Reach decline and the shift to interest-based distribution**
- Van der Blom's 2026 Algorithm Insights Report (7th edition) is described as based on 1.3 million posts from about 50,000 creators in H1 2026; headline: reach down ~60% for active creators over two years, engagement down only ~20-25% — [Creator Science podcast, ep. 307](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/richard-van-der-blom-2/); [Castfox episode listing](https://www.castfox.net/podcast/creator-science-with-jay-clouse-111713/episode/307-richard-van-der-blom-the-state-of-linkedin-in-2026-based-on-data-from-13-million-posts-2124510)
- Another summary of the same report series gives views -50%, engagement -25%, follower growth -59%; a third cites 1.8 million posts. Post counts and percentages are inconsistent across secondary coverage — [Melanie Goodman Substack](https://melaniegoodmanlinkedinconsultant.substack.com/p/linkedin-algorithm-2026-reach-topic-authority); [Perroly](https://www.perroly.com/blog/linkedin-organic-2026-what-works); [Behind Good Content #31](https://podscan.fm/podcasts/behind-good-content/episodes/31-richard-van-der-blom) (says ">2 million posts per year")
- Share of a post's initial distribution going to the author's connections reportedly fell from 70% (2024) to 54% (2025) to ~10% (2026) — reported in secondary coverage of van der Blom data — [Leoni Consulting Group](https://www.leoniconsultinggroup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-what-drives-reach-now); [Missive Digital](https://missivedigital.com/linkedin-algorithm-updates/)
- Company page reach reportedly ~1.6% of followers (supports focusing on the personal profile, as Omar does) — [Endatasite marketing insights](https://marketing-insights.endatasite.com/articles/linkedin-algorithm-2026-company-pages-video-b2b-reach)
- March 12, 2026: LinkedIn Engineering reportedly published the architecture of a rebuilt feed using a single LLM-based retrieval system and a sequential ranking model over 1,000+ past member interactions; posts are matched to readers by topic, author profile and reader history — [Upgrowth fact check](https://upgrowth.in/linkedin-algorithm-2026-360brew-update/); [SocialPilot, Sept 2026 update](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm)
- LinkedIn's Tim Jurka is paraphrased as saying the feed is deliberately pulling back from repetitive, click-driven posts toward relevant/meaningful content; LinkedIn has said it prioritizes posts from connections and expert, knowledge-led content over virality (third-party paraphrase, not verified quote) — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/science-technology/with-this-linkedin-algorithm-change-your-best-posts-could/470219); [Alpha FMC](https://alphafmc.com/insights/whos-doing-the-talking-on-linkedin/)
- Entrepreneur headline reports the change means "your best posts could reach new readers for months" (longer post lifespan for relevant content) — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/science-technology/with-this-linkedin-algorithm-change-your-best-posts-could/470219)
- "360Brew" conflict: some sources say it powers the live feed; others quote Jurka saying it was an internal model tested with a small group and shut down, not used in ranking. - Unconfirmed single-source claim: in Oct 2026 Jurka said LinkedIn runs "AI-slop detection" on posts distributed beyond a user's network — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm)

**Format performance**
- Socialinsider 2026 benchmark (1.3M posts, 16,645 business pages — note: company pages, not personal profiles): native document/carousel 7.0% avg engagement, text-only 4.5%, external-link posts 3.25% (lowest) — [Just Pollen, LinkedIn engagement 2026](https://www.justpollen.com/blog/linkedin-engagement-study-2026); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/report-shows-document-posts-on-linkedin-see-more-engagement/816551/)
- Document carousels described as the only format whose performance is still rising (blog claim, not verified against report) — [Carousels Generator, algorithm 2026](https://carousels-generator.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-carousels); [Carousel stats 2026](https://carousels-generator.com/blog/linkedin-carousel-statistics-2026)
- Video "under the most pressure"; video views falling while engagement holds — [Endatasite](https://marketing-insights.endatasite.com/articles/linkedin-algorithm-2026-company-pages-video-b2b-reach); [Perroly](https://www.perroly.com/blog/linkedin-organic-2026-what-works)
- Polls: mixed — one source says highest reach but lowest engagement; another says 22% fewer impressions than photo posts — [Melanie Goodman](https://melaniegoodmanlinkedinconsultant.substack.com/p/linkedin-algorithm-2026-reach-topic-authority); [Leoni Consulting](https://www.leoniconsultinggroup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-what-drives-reach-now)
- Metricool LinkedIn study (577,000+ posts): posts with links got 13.57% more interactions and 4.90% more views than posts without — directly contradicts the "links kill reach" narrative — [Metricool LinkedIn trends](https://metricool.com/linkedin-trends/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/linkedin-study-finds-adding-links-boosts-engagement-by-13/544524/); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/linkedin-posts-with-links-boost-engagement-new-study/)

**External links and "link in first comment"**
- LinkedIn's Sr. Director of Product Management reportedly said there is no algorithmic penalty for posts with links if the post delivers value on its own (secondhand via Matt Navarra; original LinkedIn post not opened) — [Threads, Matt Navarra](https://www.threads.com/@mattnavarra/post/DOWa_61Cown/linkedin-says-posts-with-links-are-not-intentionally-penalized-for-reachlinkedin?hl=en); [ConnectSafely, May 2026](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/how-to-add-link-to-linkedin-post-guide-2026)
- One summary of van der Blom data: one external link in the post body reduces median reach by 18.8%; also claims links in comments are now suppressed (up to -80% visibility) — the comment claim is unverified — [Melanie Goodman](https://melaniegoodmanlinkedinconsultant.substack.com/p/linkedin-algorithm-2026-reach-topic-authority)
- Agorapulse experiment concluded LinkedIn does not punish posts with links in comments, but also that the workaround offers no clear advantage — [Agorapulse Social Media Lab](https://www.agorapulse.com/social-media-lab/linkedin-comments-link/)
- Claimed 40-60% reach cuts for URL posts circulate without primary sourcing — [Gromming](https://gromming.com/blog/linkedin-external-links-penalty); [FeedBoss](https://www.feedboss.ai/blog/do-linkedin-links-reduce-reach); skeptical review: [Amit Blogwala](https://www.amitblogwala.com/linkedin-myths-keeping-experts-invisible/)
- Most credible explanation of the link-post gap is behavioral (users leave the platform, less dwell), not a hard-coded penalty — [FeedBoss](https://www.feedboss.ai/blog/do-linkedin-links-reduce-reach); [ConnectSafely](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/how-to-add-link-to-linkedin-post-guide-2026)

### Inferences
- Omar's current pipeline (every blog post becomes a link-card post) uses the format that benchmarks rank lowest for engagement. It should not be the only format: keep link posts but make the text stand alone (full insight in the post, link as "read more"), and add native formats (PDF carousel summarizing the article, text-only posts with one data point) that do not depend on the click.
- Given interest-based distribution, topical consistency matters more than network size: posts should stay within a small set of clearly identifiable expertise topics (IA, blockchain/fintech, marketing digital, MiPyMEs) so the LLM ranker associates his profile with them.
- "Link in first comment" is not worth adopting as a rule; his panel's link-card approach is acceptable if the post body delivers value. A reasonable test: alternate link-in-body vs. no-link native posts and compare in LinkedIn analytics over 8-12 weeks.
- LinkedIn's Posts API (which his `linkedin-publicar` function uses) can publish documents/images, so carousels could later be added to the panel pipeline (implementation detail outside this research; not verified here).

- No LinkedIn first-party documentation retrieved on dwell time or comments as ranking signals.

## 2. Post length, hooks, posting days/times for Mexico, frequency for personal profiles

### Takeaway
The best-supported length is ~1,300-2,500 characters with a strong first ~140-210 characters before "ver más". Timing data is contradictory (Buffer 2026 now favors 3-8 pm mid/late week; Hootsuite favors early mornings; Metricool shows activity peak 9-12), and none is Mexico-specific, so Omar should test in his own analytics. Frequency guidance (2-5/week) lacks solid evidence.

### Cited Findings
- AuthoredUp analysis of 372,126 posts (Sept 2025-Feb 2026): 1,301-2,500 character band earned ~27% higher engagement than posts under 400 characters; best band 2,001-2,500; no sharp drop near the 3,000 cap — [Heistbrain benchmarks](https://heistbrain.com/benchmarks/linkedin-post-length.html)
- Taplio benchmark: engagement rises steadily with length, favors 2,000+ characters — [Taplio](https://taplio.com/blog/how-long-should-a-linkedin-post-be); other guides recommend 1,200-1,600 or even 600-1,200 (conflicting) — [ConnectSafely](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/ideal-linkedin-post-length-engagement-guide-2026); [ViralBrain](https://www.viralbrain.ai/blog/ideal-linkedin-post-length)
- "See more" truncation at roughly 140-210 characters depending on device; hook must fit there — [LinkedinPreview](https://linkedinpreview.com/blog/linkedin-post-length-guide-2026)
- Buffer 2026 (4.8M posts): peak engagement moved later, 3-8 pm; top slots Wed 4 pm, Fri 3 pm, Fri 4 pm; Wednesday best day, then Thu and Fri; Mon and Tue lowest. In 2025 the peak was office hours — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/)
- Hootsuite (1M+ posts, secondhand): early mornings, e.g., Tue 6-8 am, Wed 9 am; Sprout Social (secondhand): Tue-Thu 11 am-5 pm — summarized in [Postfast](https://postfa.st/blog/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin); [WsCubeTech](https://www.wscubetech.com/blog/?p=19366)
- Metricool LinkedIn Study 2026 (673,658 posts, 63,108 accounts): 9:00-12:00 is when most posts are published; the study itself warns not to copy that window blindly and to check one's own audience — [Metricool, mejor hora (ES)](https://metricool.com/best-time-to-post-social-networks/)
- Spanish-language guides: Tue-Thu 9-10 am and 12-2 pm ([Snov.io](https://snov.io/blog/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/)); Merca2.0 has a Mexico-oriented guide ([Merca2.0](https://www.merca20.com/cual-es-la-mejor-hora-para-publicar-en-linkedin/)); Euroinnova says to schedule for the audience's local workday ([Euroinnova](https://articulos.euroinnova.com/empresarial/mejor-hora-para-publicar-en-linkedin))
- Frequency: 2-5 posts/week for personal profiles recommended without a cited study — [Whitehat SEO](https://whitehat-seo.co.uk/blog/increase-engagement-on-linkedin)

### Inferences
- For Omar (Culiacán, Sinaloa is UTC-7, one hour behind CDMX; most Mexican business audience on CDMX time): a reasonable starting test is Tue-Thu either ~8-9 am or ~4-6 pm Sinaloa time (9-10 am / 5-7 pm CDMX), then keep whichever wins in his analytics. Avoid Monday.
- A sustainable cadence given he approves each post manually and has 4 blog posts: 2 LinkedIn posts/week (1 linked to a blog article, 1 native: carousel, data point, or class/research observation) is within the commonly recommended range and realistic.
- Spanish text runs ~15-20% longer than English; the 1,300-2,000 character range fits a well-developed idea in Spanish.

### Gaps
- No Mexico- or LatAm-specific timing study found; no reliable LinkedIn user count for Mexico 2026 found.
- No rigorous data isolating hook quality or ideal frequency for personal profiles.

## 4. SEO for Spanish blog content in 2026 with AI Overviews

### Takeaway
AI Overviews cut clicks substantially on informational queries (Ahrefs: -58% CTR for position 1; Pew: 8% vs 15% click rate), with partial recovery in early 2026 and better results for pages cited in the Overview and for branded queries. Generic explainers are the most exposed; original research and first-hand expertise are the content types still earning links and AI citations.

### Cited Findings
- Pew (68,000 queries): users clicked results 8% of the time when an AI summary appeared vs 15% without — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/impact-of-ai-overviews-how-publishers-need-to-adapt/556843/)
- Ahrefs (Dec 2025 snapshot, 300,000 keywords): AI Overview presence correlates with 58% lower CTR for the top-ranking page; ~19% lower at position 10 — [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/02/223-google-ai-overviews-click-through-rates-58-study/); [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update)
- Seer Interactive (Apr 2026 update): CTR on AIO queries bottomed at 1.3% (Dec 2025), recovered to 2.4% (Feb 2026), still below ~3.3% on non-AIO queries; pages cited in the AIO get more clicks than uncited pages — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-ctr-recovery-study-475566)
- Branded queries with AIO: +18% CTR (Amsive, secondhand) — [Cognizo](https://www.cognizo.ai/blog/google-ai-overviews-statistics)
- Randomized field experiment: 39.8% drop in outbound clicks attributable to AIO, concentrated almost entirely on informational queries — [Keywords Everywhere tracker](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/ai-overviews/); [Slate HQ](https://slatehq.com/blog/google-ai-overviews-statistics)
- SparkToro: zero-click searches at 68% of US Google queries Jan-Apr 2026 — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-zero-click-searches-2026-study-479717)
- Seer Interactive (247 blog posts, 2 years): organic and AI-referral traffic correlate strongly; author argues original research earns more AI referrals than listicles/guides (small sample) — [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/where-organic-search-ai-traffic-behave-the-same-and-where-they-diverge-a-study-of-2-years-of-blog-data)
- Original research earns links because journalists/bloggers cite it (vendor example) — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/hub/seo/original-research)
- Older (pre-2025) length data: content >3,000 words earned 77.2% more referring domains than <1,000 words (912M posts, Backlinko/BuzzSumo); Semrush: 14+ word headlines and H2/H3 structure performed better — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/more-blog-traffic); [Shno compilation](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/content-seo-statistics) — flag: older data
- Ahrefs (Dec 2023, old): 96.55% of pages get zero Google organic traffic — [Shno compilation](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/content-seo-statistics)
- E-E-A-T/experience (third-party interpretations of Google guidance): Google wants evidence the author has done what they write about (case studies, original photos, lessons from failure); helpful content system folded into core ranking in March 2024 and evaluates sites as a whole; AI as drafting assistant is acceptable if content is people-first — [Hive Digital](https://www.hivedigital.com/2023/12/20/what-google-wants-from-your-content/); [SEO Sherpa](https://seosherpa.com/google-ai-search-guidelines/); [V9 Digital](https://www.v9digital.com/insights/e-e-a-t-in-2025-how-googles-updated-guidelines-affect-your-seo-strategy/)
- AI-written content: mixed results (39% saw traffic increase, 34% no change; survey on 20,000 URLs) — [MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2025/52670/can-ai-written-blog-posts-rank-well-on-google-search)

### Inferences
- For omarvaldez.com, the content least exposed to AI Overviews and most aligned with E-E-A-T: (a) original data from his own research (FCA-UAS surveys of Sinaloa SMEs, student/seminar data in aggregate), (b) first-hand consulting cases ("lo que vi al implementar X en una PyME"), (c) local angles (Sinaloa/México regulation, SAT, CNBV/fintech law, Buen Fin data), (d) opinion/analysis signed by an SNII researcher. Generic "qué es blockchain" explainers will mostly be answered by AIO.
- Brand queries benefit from AIO; building "Omar Valdez" as a searched name (via LinkedIn, classes, newsletter) protects traffic.
- Publishing frequency: no 2026 data found; given a 4-post base and site-wide quality evaluation, 2 strong articles/month (1,500-2,500 words with sources, author bio, dates, charts) is a defensible inference over weekly thin posts.
- Technical E-E-A-T on his site: author box with SNII level and institutional affiliation, `Article`/`Person` structured data, visible publish/update dates, references section. (Inference; schema impact on AIO not verified here.)

## 5. Content pillars, editorial calendar frameworks, repurposing, newsletter integration

### Takeaway
